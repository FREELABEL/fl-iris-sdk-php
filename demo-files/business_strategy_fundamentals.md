# Business Strategy Fundamentals

## Course Introduction

Welcome to Business Strategy Fundamentals - a practical course designed to equip leaders with the tools and frameworks needed to develop and execute winning strategies.

## Part 1: Understanding Strategy

### What is Strategy?
Strategy is the art and science of making choices about:
- **Where to Play**: Which markets, segments, and geographies to compete in
- **How to Win**: What unique value proposition will differentiate you
- **Core Capabilities**: What you must be excellent at to deliver value

### The Strategy Cascade (Roger Martin & A.G. Lafley)
1. Winning Aspiration - What does winning look like?
2. Where to Play - Which customers and markets?
3. How to Win - What's your competitive advantage?
4. Capabilities - What must you be great at?
5. Management Systems - How will you support the strategy?

## Part 2: Industry Analysis

### Porter's Five Forces
Analyze competitive intensity through:

| Force | Key Questions |
|-------|---------------|
| Rivalry | How intense is current competition? |
| New Entrants | How easy is it for new players to enter? |
| Substitutes | What alternatives exist for customers? |
| Buyer Power | How much leverage do customers have? |
| Supplier Power | How dependent are you on suppliers? |

### Blue Ocean Strategy
Instead of competing in crowded markets (red oceans), create uncontested market space (blue oceans) by:
- **Eliminating** factors the industry takes for granted
- **Reducing** factors below industry standards
- **Raising** factors above industry standards
- **Creating** factors the industry has never offered

## Part 3: Competitive Advantage

### Three Generic Strategies (Michael Porter)
1. **Cost Leadership**: Lowest cost producer in the industry
2. **Differentiation**: Unique products/services commanding premium prices
3. **Focus**: Targeting a narrow market segment

### Sources of Sustainable Advantage
- Proprietary technology or IP
- Network effects and platform dynamics
- Brand equity and customer loyalty
- Economies of scale or scope
- Unique organizational culture
- Strategic assets and locations

## Part 4: Growth Strategies

### Ansoff Matrix

|                  | Existing Products | New Products |
|------------------|-------------------|--------------|
| Existing Markets | Market Penetration | Product Development |
| New Markets      | Market Development | Diversification |

### Organic vs. Inorganic Growth
**Organic Growth:**
- Internal R&D and innovation
- Sales force expansion
- New market entry

**Inorganic Growth:**
- Mergers & Acquisitions
- Strategic partnerships
- Joint ventures
- Licensing agreements

## Part 5: Strategy Execution

### The Balanced Scorecard
Measure performance across four perspectives:
1. **Financial**: Revenue, profit, ROI
2. **Customer**: Satisfaction, retention, market share
3. **Internal Processes**: Quality, efficiency, innovation
4. **Learning & Growth**: Employee skills, culture, technology

### OKRs (Objectives and Key Results)
- **Objectives**: Qualitative, inspiring goals
- **Key Results**: Quantitative, measurable outcomes
- Typically set quarterly with 3-5 KRs per objective
- Aim for 70% achievement (stretch goals)

## Part 6: Case Studies

### Case Study 1: Apple's Ecosystem Strategy
How Apple created a walled garden that increases customer lifetime value and switching costs through:
- Hardware integration (iPhone, Mac, Watch, AirPods)
- Software ecosystem (iOS, macOS, iCloud)
- Services revenue (App Store, Apple Music, TV+)

### Case Study 2: Amazon's Flywheel
Jeff Bezos's virtuous cycle:
Lower Prices → More Customers → More Sellers → Better Selection → Better Experience → More Traffic → Lower Costs per Unit → Lower Prices...

### Case Study 3: Netflix's Pivot
From DVD-by-mail to streaming to content creation - a masterclass in strategic transformation while maintaining core customer focus.
